The cut: what I know and what I don't

What the seller says: cut the yellow-marked trace, then feed 5 V to the VCC pad. This separates the USB socket's power from the board's 3.3 V rail, so the chip stays at 3.3 V and the dongle gets 5 V.

What I could not confirm: none of the listing photos I saw shows the yellow mark, so I can't point to the exact trace. It should be a thin trace or a solder-bridge near the VCC pad next to the USB socket (on the back of the board the pad is labelled VBUS). Find it on your board before cutting, and send me a close-up photo of that corner if you are unsure. It might also not be cuttable; the fallback is at the end.

Before you power it

  1. Set a multimeter to continuity. Touch the VCC pad and the board's 3.3V pad. If they beep, the two are tied together: that is the trace to cut.
  2. Cut only that trace (a hobby knife, two or three cuts). Check again: VCC and 3.3V must no longer beep.
  3. Solder a wire from the XIAO's 5V pin to the VCC pad. Wire everything else from the table.
  4. Power it from the Mac and measure with the board connected: the 3.3V pad reads about 3.3 V, the VCC pad about 5 V, and SCLK, MOSI, MISO, SS, INT and RST never read 5 V.

If anything reads 5 V on a signal pad, unplug it and send me the readings. Never connect the VCC pad to 3.3 V, and never feed 5 V into a board whose 3.3V pad is still tied to VCC.

If the trace cannot be cut

Do not feed 5 V into the VCC pad then. Instead, power the dongle through a separate USB-A breakout wired to the XIAO's 5V pin, or lift the USB socket's power leg. Tell me what you find and I will adapt the plan.
