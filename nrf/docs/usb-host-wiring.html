The yellow line: I could not find it, and it may not be needed

The seller says to cut a yellow-marked wire and then feed 5 V to the VCC pad. None of the listing photos shows a yellow mark. A manual for a similar mini board says the opposite: its USB power link is a solder jumper that ships open, and you close it. I can't tell which case your board is, so let a multimeter decide when it arrives. Nothing needs cutting until a test says so.

Two continuity tests (multimeter on the beep setting, board unpowered)

  1. VCC pad to the 3.3V pad. No beep: nothing ties them, so do not cut anything. Beep: they are tied together, and that link is the "yellow wire". Cut only that link, then test again: it must not beep.
  2. VCC pad to the four solder pins of the USB-A socket. One of them is VBUS. Beep on one pin: the pad feeds the socket, so 5 V on the VCC pad powers the dongle. No beep on any: the power link is an open solder jumper near the socket (labelled VBUS on the back); bridge it with a small blob of solder, then test again.

If the results do not match either case, stop and send me a photo of both sides of the board with the meter readings. Do not guess.

Before you power it

  1. Do the two tests above and make the change they call for, if any.
  2. Solder a wire from the XIAO's 5V pin to the VCC pad. Wire everything else from the table.
  3. Power it from the Mac and measure: the 3.3V pad about 3.3 V, the VCC pad about 5 V, and SCLK, MOSI, MISO, SS, INT and RST never 5 V.

If anything reads 5 V on a signal pad, unplug it and send me the readings. Never feed 5 V into the VCC pad while it is still tied to the 3.3V pad.

If it can't be made to work

Power the dongle through a separate USB-A breakout wired to the XIAO's 5V pin, or use a board with a documented 5 V input such as Adafruit's USB Host FeatherWing. Tell me what you find and I will adapt the plan.
